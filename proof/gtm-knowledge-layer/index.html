Selected work / Sales knowledge

Answers sellers can trust

I helped bring scattered sales knowledge together and built the agent sellers could use to find answers across it.

The problem

Sales guidance lived in separate spaces, with no clear authority. Product documentation was inaccessible to reps. Finding an answer often meant finding a colleague.

How I approached it

I helped consolidate the knowledge base, decided which sources belonged and established who could maintain them. The agent needed dependable material behind its answers.

What I built

The Sales Knowledge Agent, its source and specialist instructions, automated documentation publishing, and the access rules supporting the work.

The result

Sellers could read shared guidance or ask across it. Previously inaccessible documentation became available, with publishing and editing rules to keep it maintained.

How the work came together

01Agree on the sourcesConsolidation / ownership / access+

Sales guidance was spread across a knowledge base, onboarding space, legacy sales space and cold-call grading space. I helped consolidate those sources and defined which spaces should remain, which material should move and which spaces should retire.

I set rules for who could contribute and edit, and how new agents would be created. The scope included sales processes, onboarding, stage definitions, buying signals, product information and selected technical references.

The next gap: organizing the sales material did not make the product documentation accessible. It still lived in an engineering repository sellers could not open.

02Make missing knowledge accessibleProduct publishing / partner content / support+

I built a publisher that brought 237 product-documentation pages into the sales workspace and synchronized changes daily. It used the documentation’s table of contents to select content, rather than exposing everything in the engineering repository.

Full page paths prevented duplicate filenames from overwriting each other. Links were published only once their destination existed. Unchanged pages were left alone, preserving useful history.

Partner content published to the portal only when explicitly labeled partner-facing. The support knowledge base was rebuilt from the same product-documentation source.

A build decision: I rejected a sync app after testing because its required matching marker appeared on every page. The publisher also cut a quiet run from about eighteen minutes rewriting 237 pages to about three minutes with no page writes.

The next gap: the information was available, but a seller still needed to know where to look.

03Build the Sales Knowledge AgentQuestions across sources / citations / specialist instructions+

I built the Sales Knowledge Agent on top of the consolidated sales knowledge base. It gave sellers a way to ask questions across the approved material instead of knowing which space or page held the answer.

I wrote instructions requiring it to use configured sources, avoid the open web and cite only links found in those sources. If the answer was missing, it was to explain what it had checked and what documentation was needed.

A product-documentation specialist was instructed to quote the source, keeping the conditions and limitations attached to a feature. I reviewed the technical specialist’s sources and corrected a conflicting instruction that had been blocking product questions.

The next check: useful answers depended on both the source material selected and who could change it.

04Review what can shape an answerSource review / disclosure / editing permissions+

I reviewed the technical specialist’s 19 engineering sources and removed one describing an internal toggle and an unsupported configuration. I added instructions about what was appropriate to disclose from the remaining material.

I also checked actual permissions against the intended audience. The agent was available more broadly than planned, but those users already had read access to its sources. The more consequential issue was who could edit the pages feeding its answers.

I tightened editing rights in the sales knowledge base and made the published documentation space view-and-comment only, verifying the settings through the API.

Why that mattered: changes to a source page could change what a seller was told. Maintaining the knowledge base required control over those changes as well as instructions for the agent.
