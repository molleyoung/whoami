Knowledge + intelligence

Answers sellers
can trust

Sales guidance was scattered across documentation sources. Reps needed to find the right answer, understand which guidance to trust and use it in their work.

My contribution

I helped consolidate the sales knowledge base, built the Sales Knowledge Agent, and developed the publishing and access rules behind it.

The problem

Finding an answer depended on finding a person.

Sales processes, onboarding material and technical guidance lived in separate spaces. There was no clear rule about which one was authoritative. Product documentation added another gap: it lived in an engineering repository sellers could not access.

Reps asked colleagues instead. The answer depended on whom they reached and what that person remembered. Adding a search tool would still leave the team searching through conflicting guidance and missing material.

Bring the knowledge together

Establish where the team should look for answers.

I helped consolidate the sales knowledge base across disparate documentation sources. I defined which spaces should remain, which material should be brought together and which legacy spaces should retire. I also set rules for who could contribute, edit and create new agents.

The work covered guidance for selling and operating: onboarding, sales processes, stage definitions, buying signals, product information and selected technical material. Product documentation was one part of the knowledge base.

Build the agent

Help reps find the answer across those sources.

I built the seller-facing knowledge agent on top of the sales knowledge base. I wrote its instructions to use configured sources, stay off the open web and cite only links found in those sources. When an answer was missing, it was to explain what it checked and what documentation was needed.

I added a product-documentation specialist and reviewed the technical specialist’s source list. The product specialist was instructed to quote the documentation so a feature’s conditions and limitations would stay attached to the answer. I also corrected a conflicting parent-agent instruction that had been preventing product questions from being answered.

01

Show the source

Use approved material and cite its pages. Do not browse the web or invent a supporting link.

02

Keep the qualifications

Quote product documentation so the conditions and limits stay attached to a capability.

03

Say what is missing

When the sources do not answer a question, explain what was checked and what documentation is needed.

Maintain the information behind the answer

Keep updates connected to the original source.

I built a publisher to bring 237 product-documentation pages into the sales workspace and synchronize changes daily. It follows the documentation’s own table of contents, uses full page paths to prevent name collisions and leaves unchanged pages alone.

That preserves useful page history and avoids maintaining a separate sales copy by hand. The same product-documentation source also supplied the rebuilt support knowledge base. Approved content reached the partner portal through an explicit partner-facing label.

The first sync tool required a matching marker that appeared visibly on every page. I rejected it after testing and built the publisher instead.

The replacement also addressed silent page overwrites from duplicate filenames and links that could accidentally create draft pages. A run with no source changes went from rewriting all 237 pages in about eighteen minutes to writing nothing in about three minutes.

Review what can become an answer

Source selection and editing rights mattered as much as the prompt.

During a review of the technical specialist’s sources, I removed internal material that described an unsupported configuration and added instructions about what was appropriate to disclose. I checked actual permissions as well as the intended audience.

I tightened editing access in the sales knowledge base and set the published documentation space to view and comment only, verifying the settings through the API. Changes to the material feeding an agent can change its answers, so the write permissions needed attention too.

Alongside the sales knowledge work, I documented field meanings, metric definitions and data reliability in a GTM data catalog. That gave analysis and AI a reference for interpreting business data while the sales knowledge base supplied the guidance sellers needed.

What changed

A shared reference for sellers. A way to keep it useful.

For the sales team

Scattered guidance came together. Previously inaccessible documentation became available. Reps could read the source or ask the agent, with instructions to return supporting pages and acknowledge missing answers.

For the people maintaining it

Content had a defined home, editing rules and audience boundaries. Product updates reached sales, partners and support through publishing workflows, while unchanged pages kept their useful history.
