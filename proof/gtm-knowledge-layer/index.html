Knowledge + intelligence / flagship story

GTM Knowledge Layer

A governed foundation that separates product and business knowledge from operational data definitions, then makes both usable by people, analysis, automation, and AI.

My role

Source-of-truth design · knowledge architecture · publishing · governance · delivery

Starting condition

Knowledge existed everywhere except where work happened.

Sales knowledge had no usable centralized foundation. Product truth, sales guidance, operating rules, and data definitions lived across different systems, formats, and owners.

The risk was not just search friction. Without provenance, audience boundaries, or interpretation rules, the same source could produce inconsistent answers for a seller, analyst, partner, customer, or agent.

Architecture

Two knowledge lanes. One governance contract.

The architecture keeps operational data meaning distinct from product and business content. Governance creates a trusted context layer, then delivery interfaces adapt it to the audience and task.

Source model

Authority is designed, not assumed.

Each source type needs an explicit purpose, owner, review path, audience, and delivery contract. That prevents a convenient copy from quietly becoming a new source of truth.

01

Product sources

Approved documentation supplies product capability, behavior, and implementation context.

02

Sales sources

Curated guidance supplies positioning, discovery, process, and field-facing answers.

03

Operational sources

Systems such as Salesforce supply current records, process state, and accountable ownership.

04

Data definitions

Versioned catalog content explains objects, fields, metrics, relationships, and caveats.

05

Audience boundaries

Seller, partner, customer, analyst, and machine contexts receive only appropriate content.

06

Delivery contracts

Every interface knows what it can retrieve, how freshness is represented, and where to escalate ambiguity.

Publishing path

Knowledge becomes useful through a controlled release loop.

The publishing model is intentionally simple: identify authority, review changes, publish an approved version, synchronize it to a delivery layer, observe use, and feed corrections back to the source.

  1. 01

    Source

    Identify the authoritative product, sales, operational, or data definition.

    owner + provenance
  2. 02

    Review

    Check meaning, audience, accuracy, and conflicts before release.

    validation + approval
  3. 03

    Publish

    Create a stable, versioned expression of approved knowledge.

    version + timestamp
  4. 04

    Synchronize

    Move approved content into governed delivery surfaces and indexes.

    access + freshness
  5. 05

    Consume

    Serve the right context to humans, analysis, automation, and agents.

    audience + task
  6. 06

    Correct

    Return gaps, conflicts, and feedback to the responsible source.

    issue + owner + revision

Governance

The controls are part of the product.

Trust comes from making boundaries visible. The layer needs to say where an answer came from, who can receive it, how current it is, and what happens when sources disagree.

Provenance

Trace every answer

Keep source identity and lineage available for review and correction.

Validation

Review before release

Check content against authoritative owners and explicit operating definitions.

Permissions

Respect access

Deliver only the knowledge appropriate to the user's role and channel.

Freshness

Expose currency

Represent versions, update cadence, and stale content instead of hiding them.

Boundaries

Separate audiences

Keep internal guidance, partner material, and customer-facing truth distinct.

Interpretation

Encode the caveats

Give people and machines the rules needed to use definitions correctly.

Application

The Sales Knowledge Agent is one interface, not a separate source.

The agent retrieves from the governed foundation and translates approved context into a seller-facing answer. It does not become the owner of product truth or invent a parallel sales knowledge base.

Molly owns the knowledge foundation and delivery design. IT remains appropriately scoped to connector, identity, permission, and access implementation where required.

Approved knowledge + data definitions→Governed retrieval + Sales Knowledge Agent→Seller answer + source context
Gap, conflict, or ambiguity→ source owner review and correction
