Sales knowledge / case study

Answers sellers
can trust.

Sales knowledge was scattered across separate spaces. Some of the documentation reps needed was out of reach entirely. I helped consolidate that knowledge and built the Sales Knowledge Agent so sellers could ask questions across it.

My work included knowledge organization, agent design, automated publishing, source review and editing permissions.

01Start with the knowledge

The answer depended on whom a rep asked.

Sales processes, onboarding material and technical guidance lived in different spaces, without a clear rule about which one to trust. Product documentation lived in an engineering repository sellers could not open. Reps asked colleagues instead and got what that person remembered.

I helped consolidate the sales knowledge base and set the decisions behind it: which spaces would stay, which material would move, which legacy spaces would retire and who could maintain the content. That work covered the guidance sellers needed to do their jobs, from sales stages and buying signals to product and technical questions.

Bringing the material together established where the team should look. It also exposed what was still missing.

02Close the access gap

Make the source material available and keep it connected.

I built a publisher to bring 237 product-documentation pages into the sales workspace and synchronize changes daily. Sellers gained access to the documentation without someone having to keep a separate sales copy up to date by hand.

I tested a sync app first, but its required matching marker appeared as text on every page. Building the publisher also let me address problems the sync needed to handle properly: duplicate filenames overwriting pages, links creating unwanted drafts and unchanged content being rewritten on every run.

The publisher followed the documentation’s own table of contents and left unchanged pages alone. Page history could show actual edits again.

Less maintenance, useful history. A run with no source changes went from rewriting all 237 pages in about eighteen minutes to writing nothing in about three.
03Build the agent

Reps should not have to know which page holds the answer.

With the knowledge base in place, I built the Sales Knowledge Agent across its approved sources. A seller could ask a question about the sales process or product and get an answer tied back to the supporting documentation.

I wrote the agent’s instructions and added specialist guidance where accuracy required more care. For product questions, the specialist was told to quote the documentation so a feature’s conditions and limits stayed attached. When the sources did not contain an answer, the agent was instructed to explain what it had checked and what documentation was needed.

I also corrected a conflicting instruction that had been preventing product questions from being answered. Making the content accessible and making the agent use it correctly were both part of the build.

04Review and extend

The work continued behind the answers.

Reviewing the technical specialist’s sources, I found internal material describing an unsupported configuration. I removed it and added instructions about what was appropriate to disclose from the remaining sources.

I checked permissions too. The agent’s audience was broader than intended, but those people already had access to its source pages. The more consequential issue was who could edit those pages and change what the agent might tell a seller. I tightened write access in the sales knowledge base and made the published documentation space view-and-comment only, verifying the settings through the API.

I extended the publishing work to partners, with an explicit label controlling which content reached the portal. The support knowledge base was rebuilt from the same product-documentation source. Updates could serve three audiences without each team maintaining its own version of the original material.

What changed for the team

A shared place to find an answer.

Sellers gained access to guidance that had been scattered or unavailable, and an agent that could help them find it. The people maintaining that knowledge had clear decisions about where content belonged, who could change it and what could be shared with partners.

The result combined the knowledge base, the agent and the ongoing work behind both: publishing updates, preserving source detail and reviewing what could become a sales answer.
