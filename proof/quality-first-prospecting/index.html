Signal-Based
Prospecting

I built the prospecting strategy and the motion to execute it.

I defined which accounts to pursue, which people to reach, what signals warranted action, and how reps would engage. I built the targeting, sourcing, enrichment, signal architecture, and seller workflows, then used performance data and rep feedback to improve the approach.

Each vendor kept its own version of intent and engagement in its own fields. A rep had no single place to see why an account mattered right now, while low connection rates made reaching the right people difficult.

Why it mattered

Prospecting capacity depends on working relevant accounts and reaching the right people. Poor targeting consumes seller time and enrichment credits; disconnected signals and routing gaps can delay relevant follow-up and limit pipeline creation.

Evidence

What changed

Reps spent less time finding leads and more time in conversations. Prepared lists reduced sourcing work, pickup-likelihood scores helped prioritize calls, and enriched leads supplied context and talking points for when someone answered.

Less time finding leads

Prospects arrived ready to work

Reps could request prospects from an account or use prepared weekly call lists. I connected targeting, sourcing, persona validation, enrichment, matching and ownership, with verified contact details carried into the sequencing platform and dialer.

More connections per dial

Prioritize people likely to answer

I incorporated TitanX phone-number scores into the prospecting motion so reps could prioritize by pickup likelihood. In the Orum analysis, the highest-scored tier connected at 10.32%, compared with 3.98% for unrated contacts: approximately 2.6 times the connect rate.

Better prepared when someone answered

Context and talking points alongside the lead

I enriched intent leads with account and buyer context, the reason they had surfaced, and relevant talking points. Command centers, record views, alerts and role-specific digests helped reps see whom to work and why.

Performance and improvement

Problems could be traced to their cause

Yield analysis located losses at title and persona matching. Rep feedback changed targeting, quality audits identified dropped signals and routing gaps, and credit-use planning made sourcing costs visible. Replacing an AI step with a fixed rule eliminated that step’s credit cost.

Pickup-likelihood scoring / observed results

Higher-scored contacts connected more often in both dialers.

TitanX scores the likelihood that a person will answer the phone. This score helped reps prioritize calls; it is separate from the buying signals used to explain why an account matters.

Build history

How the work evolved

I started with targeting and callable lists, then connected the signals and workflows around them. Each iteration addressed what was preventing the team from using the previous one effectively.

Open a stage to inspect the decisions and implementation.

01Get the targeting right firstClassification / buyer personas / rep feedback+

I extended automatic vertical classification to use company name and website domain as well as industry, with a lock to preserve manual corrections. I split public sector into federal, state, local, and education so those accounts could route correctly.

I built target-account views, contact views by sub-vertical, and a way for reps to flag accounts that did not belong. An account-record button triggered a Floqer workflow to find prospects matching our buyer personas, research, score, validate, and create them in Salesforce. It recorded who requested the run and when, and explained when no prospects were returned.

I connected Floqer and Salesforce through unique identifiers and carried enrichment fields into the sequencing and dialing tools.

What prompted the next step: reps could source prospects one account at a time, but the development team needed callable lists at scale and connection rates were low.

Salesforce · vertical classification · manual override protection · Floqer · unique identifiers · persona validation

02Build callable lists at scaleAccount selection / enrichment / imports / tier reporting+

I ran a paid phone-data proof of concept and turned the work into a weekly process. Account selection prioritized target accounts with no recent activity, no open opportunity, and few existing contacts, ranked for each rep.

Floqer sourced people, validated titles against our personas, and required a verified work email and mobile number. TitanX scored phone numbers by the person’s likelihood of picking up, allowing reps to prioritize higher-probability calls. Weekly Salesforce imports applied duplicate checks and owner assignment, with the fields mapped into the sequencing platform and dialer.

I compared connection rates by pickup-likelihood tier within each dialer. When yield dropped, I traced the losses to title and persona matching. That directed the correction toward targeting.

I also defined the Floqer-to-Salesforce write contract: permitted fields, matching, deduplication, and source recording. For Finance, I wrote a credit policy built around forecasting and early warnings.

What prompted the next step: call lists identified people to contact, but the reason an account mattered at that moment was still scattered across vendor data.

Floqer · Salesforce · phone-data provider · sequencing and dialer mappings · tier-level connection reporting · credit forecasting

03Create a common signal historyEvent records / taxonomy / repeatable loading+

I designed and built one Salesforce record per signal event, linked to the related lead, contact, account, and opportunity. The structure preserved the signal when a lead converted to a contact.

A shared taxonomy mapped vendor terminology into defined categories and signal types. I backfilled about 9,000 website visits, research-intent events, ad clicks, event attendance records, form submissions, and live chats.

Every signal had a stable unique key, so repeat loads would not duplicate it. I built reusable loaders for additional sources. A record-triggered flow copied new signal information onto the person immediately, allowing downstream automation to respond without waiting for a batch job.

During this work, I identified a validation rule rolling back writes on leads without a source and documented it as a priority fix.

What prompted the next step: signals were now in Salesforce, but reps still needed them in their working views, and producing workflows needed a consistent way to submit them.

Salesforce signal records · shared taxonomy · unique event keys · reusable loaders · record-triggered flow · lead-to-contact continuity

04Put signals into the rep’s dayCommand centers / routing / weekly digests+

I enriched intent leads with account and buyer context, the reason they surfaced, and relevant talking points so reps were better prepared when they connected. I organized account-executive and development-rep command centers around hot accounts, hand-raisers, signal categories, ad engagement, and priority phone numbers. Contact records also displayed account-level signals and the people associated with them.

I added signal-based contact routing, starting with public-sector procurement signals. Contacts created by these workflows received a distinct alert rather than appearing to be inbound web leads. Additional signal sources could be added through configuration.

Every rep received a weekly digest, with prospecting signals and priorities tailored to their role. I published a Signal Dictionary explaining what each buying signal meant and what to do first.

For workflow builders, I published the contract for writing records and signals into Salesforce and built a Signal Intake app.

What prompted the next step: more workflows were producing signals. Inconsistent labels, source fields, and routing inputs could make useful signals disappear or reach the wrong person.

AE and SDR command centers · contact-record visibility · signal-based routing · distinct alerts · weekly digests · Signal Dictionary · Signal Intake app

05Use feedback to improve the motionQuality audits / trial design / targeting changes+

I traced dropped signals to a workflow inventing labels outside the shared taxonomy and required the producing workflow to correct them. I audited fields where tool, channel, and workflow had been mixed together, identified missing state data that caused accounts to skip territory routing, and corrected contacts marked engaged without a qualifying signal.

When evaluating another sales engagement tool, I defined what it could create or update, sender identity, opt-out handling, and routing and alerts for replies.

Separate evaluation / dialer trial

With the dialer contract ending, I ran a two-week replacement trial against agreed success criteria and a shared definition of a connect, comparing with reps still on the original dialer. I sourced the lists, configured the integration, and verified activity logging and reporting.

Overall dial-to-connect was approximately 4% in the Orum period and 6% in the Nooks period. Unrated contacts also connected more often in the Nooks period (3.98% versus 5.70%). Contact mix and enrichment changed as well, so the difference cannot be attributed to the dialer alone. Field feedback identifying IT as the primary buyer fed into persona targeting. I also replaced an AI step with a fixed rule, removing that step’s credit cost.

Further development: signal scoring and decay, identity across job changes, hosted signal loaders with a dedicated integration user, account rollups, manager views, and persona fields on leads and contacts.

Signal-quality audits · source-field rules · territory checks · agreed trial criteria · shared connection definition · persona targeting · deterministic rules
