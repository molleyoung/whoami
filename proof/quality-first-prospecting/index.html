Selected work

Signal-Based
Prospecting

The problem

Reps needed a more reliable way to decide which accounts to pursue, find the right people, and know when to reach out. Intent and engagement data were scattered across tools, and low connection rates made reaching buyers difficult. Finding a contact also wasn’t enough: reps needed to understand why that person had surfaced and what might make the conversation relevant.

How I approached it

I started with the prospecting decisions: which accounts and buyers we wanted to reach, what warranted action, and how reps should engage. I connected those decisions to sourcing and enrichment, then brought the resulting prospects and context into the tools reps used. Performance data and rep feedback informed where the approach needed to change.

What I changed

I owned the strategy, technical implementation, rep guidance, measurement, and ongoing improvement. I built the targeting and sourcing workflows, enrichment, Salesforce signal architecture, routing, and seller views. I also established the data requirements and controls needed to keep those workflows reliable.

The impact

Reps spent less time finding leads and more time in conversations. They had prospects that matched the target audience, phone scores to help prioritize calls, and account and buyer context to prepare for outreach. Reporting and rep feedback helped me identify where targeting, data quality, or follow-up needed improvement.

Choose the accounts and people to pursue

Close

I defined the account and buyer criteria behind prospecting, then built ways to apply them consistently. Account classification used company name, website domain, and industry, with manual corrections protected from being overwritten. Public-sector categories distinguished federal, state, local, and education accounts so they could route correctly.

Reps could request prospects directly from an account. The workflow researched and validated people against our buyer personas, created the records in Salesforce, and recorded who requested the run. Weekly sourcing prioritized target accounts with no recent activity, no open opportunity, and few existing contacts. Rep feedback helped refine which accounts and titles belonged in those searches.

Help reps prioritize whom to call

Close

I built a weekly process that sourced people, validated their titles, and required a verified work email and mobile number. Phone scores reflected a person’s likelihood of picking up, giving reps a way to prioritize their calls.

Floqer handled sourcing and enrichment. Salesforce imports applied matching, duplicate checks, source recording, and ownership, with the contact and enrichment fields carried into the sequencing platform and dialer.

The original priority-scored population produced 182 connects and 93 conversations across about 1,450 dials: approximately 12.5% cumulative dial-to-connect. Later results moderated toward 9% as volume increased. These figures describe the scored population, not the team-wide connect rate.

Bring the signals together

Close

I designed and built a Salesforce record for each signal event, linked to the relevant person and account, with opportunity relationships where applicable. That gave the business a shared history of activity instead of separate vendor fields.

I mapped vendor terminology into common categories and backfilled about 9,000 signals, including website visits, research intent, ad clicks, event attendance, forms, and live chats. Unique event keys prevented duplicate records when data was loaded again. Signal history survived lead conversion, and new events updated person-level fields so routing and other automation could respond.

Prepare reps for the conversation

Close

I enriched intent leads with account and buyer context, why they had surfaced, and relevant talking points. This helped reps prepare for the person they were contacting and make use of the opportunity when someone answered.

I brought that context into AE and SDR working views, contact records, routing, and alerts. Signal-generated contacts received distinct alerts so reps could distinguish them from inbound web leads. Weekly digests highlighted priorities for each role, and a Signal Dictionary explained what the signals meant and how to respond.

Use results and feedback to improve the work

Close

I measured connection rates by phone-score tier and investigated where sourcing lost otherwise relevant prospects. When yield dropped, I traced the losses to title and persona matching. Rep feedback also informed changes to buyer targeting.

I checked whether useful signals were reaching the right people. Those checks exposed inconsistent signal labels, missing territory information, and contacts marked engaged without a qualifying signal. I corrected the rules and documented requirements for workflows writing into Salesforce. Credit-use planning and early warnings helped keep the cost of sourcing visible as the process grew.
