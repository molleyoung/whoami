Closed Won Review Agent

The problem

Closed Won did not mean a deal was ready for fulfillment. As we connected Salesforce to NetSuite through Workato, missing products or contracts, conflicting dates and terms, and incorrect customer, partner, billing, or address details could move downstream and create problems for fulfillment, revenue recognition, or compensation.

The objective

Move the integration forward without waiting for a full quote-to-cash redesign. Catch known issues before the handoff, explain what needed to be corrected, and give Sales, Finance, and RevOps a shared view of why a deal was held. Keep correction and final approval with people while improving the upstream workflow.

What I built

I built the review process, automated checks, exception guidance, and Slack alerts that govern the handoff from Closed Won to fulfillment. The agent supports the review; the responsible teams correct issues and make the final approval decision.

Select a step to explore the implementation.

Python handles repeatable business checks. Claude assists with narrow interpretation and readable remediation guidance, not the approval decision.

The impact

The process allowed the Salesforce-to-NetSuite integration to move forward while upstream fixes were implemented. Fulfillment had previously taken as long as a week in some cases; in the current process, deals move to fulfillment in about one day on average. Sales, Finance, and RevOps have a shared view of what is holding a deal and what needs to happen next.

That visibility also distinguishes recurring problems from true exceptions. Review findings inform changes to fields, workflows, automation, and enablement. As those upstream changes prevent issues earlier, I narrow, revise, or remove checks. The review protects the handoff while helping improve the process behind it.

Salesforce

Define the review population

Eligible Closed Won deals are evaluated on a consistent operating cadence. The review sits between the sales milestone and the downstream handoff, where incomplete or inconsistent deal data can affect fulfillment, revenue recognition, and compensation.

Salesforce supplies the deal data; entering the review does not itself approve a deal.

Python + narrow Claude assistance

Check the current business requirements

A scheduled Python agent evaluates the deal against the current requirements. Repeatable logic handles explicit checks wherever possible.

  • Products, dates, and commercial terms
  • Contracts and signature requirements
  • Customer, partner, and billing relationships
  • Addresses and other known fulfillment requirements

Claude is used narrowly where interpretation or clearer remediation language is useful. It does not make the final approval decision.

Shared operating visibility

Make the result actionable

Slack alerts show Sales, Finance, and RevOps what passed, what failed, and what needs to be corrected. Instead of a generic rejection, the team receives specific guidance about the expectation and why the deal cannot yet move to fulfillment.

The shared view helps teams resolve the immediate issue and distinguish recurring problems from true exceptions.

Human decision

Keep approval with people

The automation supports review rather than autonomously releasing deals. People remain responsible for correcting the deal, supplying missing context, reviewing the findings, and making the final release decision.

Corrected deals run through the same checks again before approval.

Salesforce → Workato → NetSuite

Protect downstream execution

Approved deals move through Workato to NetSuite for fulfillment. The review provides a practical gate for the data and requirements that also affect revenue recognition and compensation.

This allowed the integration to progress without first redesigning the entire quote-to-cash workflow.

Correction and recheck

Fix the source, then review again

The responsible team corrects the underlying data or supplies missing context. The same checks run again after correction, so the review requirements remain consistent before a person approves the handoff.

Recurring failures inform upstream process changes. Checks are narrowed, revised, or removed as those changes reliably prevent the underlying problem.
