01 / GITLAB

Partner development during hypergrowth

Help build the motion before there was a team.

I joined GitLab before there was a dedicated partner team and worked directly with partner leadership to support the emerging reseller motion across inbound referrals, prospective partners, and outbound referrals. I helped build and document the processes behind the motion, created resources for reseller partners, and contributed to the development of a tiered reseller program.

As partner leadership built out the dedicated team, I helped create its onboarding documentation and onboard new team members into the processes we had developed together. My role then shifted from filling gaps in an emerging function to supporting an established partner organization, while continuing to train my BDR team on how to work with partners and support the reseller motion.

  • Inbound partner referrals, prospective partners, and outbound referrals
  • Reseller resources and contribution to a tiered reseller program
  • Process documentation, ownership, routing, SLAs, and partner-sourced follow-up
  • Partner-team onboarding plus BDR training and ongoing support
What changed

Early hands-on execution became documented process, partner resources, and onboarding for the dedicated team, then a repeatable way for BDR to support the reseller motion as it scaled.

03 / REMOTE

Data-driven partner operations through hypergrowth

Turn constant change into a repeatable operating model.

Remote was growing quickly, the partner function was still taking shape, and the motions continued to change with the business. I worked across the people responsible for partnerships and the participating GTM teams to turn strategy into repeatable, scalable execution. That included the data model, portfolio and territory planning, enrichment, field adoption, reporting, and the partner deal path through pipeline acceptance, with opportunity requirements, ownership, crediting, and elements of compensation design.

  • VC and PE firms, incubators, and accelerators
  • Referral, HRIS, integration, and ecosystem partners
  • Marketplace, technology, API, community, and talent partners
  • Data-driven planning, enrichment, pipeline acceptance, compensation, and measurement
What the model supported

Complex partner strategies became repeatable acquisition motions that could scale through hypergrowth, adapt as the business changed, and be measured without losing the relationships behind the numbers.

04 / BUILDKITE

Scale-ready AWS alliance operations

Build the motion to scale from day one.

I helped design the co-selling motion and owned the partner data model, portal, and internal enablement that made it usable for sellers and partners. 05 / LUMANA

Current end-to-end ownership

Own how channel works.

My scope spans partner and reseller strategy, territory and account planning, programs, prospective partner intake, the portal, lead and deal paths, commercial controls, forecasting, and reporting. I am also the primary Salesforce administrator, with hands-on ownership of the CRM configuration and automation behind this work. I connect the partner experience to the people, information, and processes needed to run the business.

I work with leadership, sellers, channel managers, Finance, enablement, developers, and vendors to understand how the motion works in practice. I test workflows, trace the data, and investigate where people make exceptions. From there, I define the direction and sequence improvements by impact, effort, and dependencies.

That approach runs through the work: deduplication before registration changes, an interim SKU mapping during product redesign, and a Closed Won review to protect fulfillment while I map the upstream fixes. Direct and channel share definitions where appropriate, with distinct relationship, ownership, commercial, and renewal requirements.

  • Prospective partner routing, HubSpot follow-up, and rules of engagement
  • Partner agreements, tiers, referral and registration paths, ownership, alerts, and SLAs
  • Partner and channel reporting across source, attribution, influence, performance, conversion, and the funnel; historical data repair, semantic definitions, and a data catalog
  • Price books, discount guardrails, payment and billing requirements, and customer / partner account relationships
  • Hardware and software records, expansion and renewal requirements, and downstream handoffs
What has improved

Partners can sign themselves up with less manual assistance. Sellers and partner managers have clearer ownership. Registrations have defined routing and disposition expectations, and order review gives Sales, Channel, Finance, and RevOps a shared place to resolve issues.
